### --[ 0 - Introduction

We gather here today to mourn. Look at them. Those precious, fragile
little allocations, the 0x20s, the 0x40s, the brave 0x90s that hold our
`struct sockaddr_in` and our humble `char` buffers. They are the backbone
of the heap, the worker bees of the process, the silent millions that
carry every packet, every pathname, every keystroke bound for a socket.

And what has glibc done? In their relentless, obsessive quest to shave
nanoseconds off a malloc hot path, they have abandoned these poor little 
chunks. We are simply astonished, dear reader, the checks are gone.
The validation is dropped. Our tiny heap denizens are wandering, naked and
unguarded, into smallbin country.

Three decisions. Four commits across two releases. That is all it took.

- bf1015fb2d / bb5a4f5295: They killed fastbin. Just killed it. The
seven smallest chunk sizes, 0x20 through 0x80, now tumble directly into
smallbin. No PROTECT_PTR. No size-index check. Just a raw, unmangled
`fd`/`bk` pair and a single backward-link check that barely glances at
them.

- e2436d6f5a: They shortened the free path for all 55 smallbin sizes.
Chunks that once passed through the unsorted bin's five integrity checks,
0x90 through 0x3F0, now skip that gate entirely. Five checks. Gone. The
unsorted bin was never a fortress, but it was something. Now there is
none of these checks between `free()` and smallbin insertion. Nothing at
all.

- 0b9210bd76: They doubled the tcache. From 7 to 16 per size bin. A wider
moat before the arena path is reached. Is this a mitigation? Perhaps.
Fewer chunks make it to the arena.

The consequences fall into two categories. Which category a chunk falls
into depends on its size. The smallest suffered most.

For 0x20-0x80, things that were impossible are now feasible. Fastbin
had teeth. Three of them:

(1) Its early return in `_int_free` meant `_int_free_merge_chunk` was
never reached. `unlink_chunk` was reachable only through
`malloc_consolidate`, triggered by a large-enough malloc, and even then it
validated `fastbin_index(chunksize(p))` against the source fastbin.
Consolidation was behind a locked door with a guard. Now the door is gone.

(2) Fastbin was singly-linked. There was no `bk` pointer. No vector for
House of Lore, no stashing unlink. Now there is a `bk`. It points into
libc. It can be corrupted.

(3) Fastbin retrieval checked `fastbin_index(chunksize(victim)) != idx`. A
post-free SIZE corruption was caught on the way out. Now smallbin
retrieval checks nothing about SIZE. Nothing.

For 0x90-0x3F0, things that were hard are now easier. These chunks
never had PROTECT_PTR. But they did pass through the unsorted bin, and the
unsorted bin had five integrity checks, five small fences that a corrupted
chunk had to clear. Since 2.41, those five fences are removed from the path.
The chunk lands in smallbin without ever being looked at.

Pre-2.41, no small chunk reached smallbin directly. 0x20-0x80 and
0x90-0x3F0 took different two-step paths. Both are now one step.
The 0x90-0x3F0 went to smallbin directly in 2.41, the 0x20-0x80 followed in 
2.43, when fastbin was gone.

0x20-0x80, the fallen fastbin class: `free()` -> fastbin (always).
Then they waited. Usually a later malloc of the same size pulled one
straight out of the fastbin, its SIZE validated by the `fastbin_index`
check on the way out. If `malloc_consolidate` fired first those little
chunks would instead be swept into the unsorted, the unsorted loop would
sort each chunk into its smallbin, and when they finally reached smallbin,
`malloc_consolidate` had validated their SIZE.

0x90-0x3F0, the unsorted travelers: `free()` -> unsorted bin (always).
Then, on the next `malloc` traversal, the unsorted loop sorted each chunk
into its smallbin. Deterministic, per-free, but still two steps. And the
five checks fired on every traversal.

By 2.43, both go to smallbin directly on `free()`. The middle step is
eliminated. The 0x20s don't wait for consolidation. The 0x90s don't pass
through validation. They just go. Straight to smallbin. Unchecked.

For the 0x20-0x80s, this is a shift from safe-linking to a weaker fallback,
PROTECT_PTR is lost on `fd`, and every arena free now enters the  merge
path (`_int_free_merge_chunk`) with no check that its SIZE matches the bin
it lands in. What used to require a consolidation now fires
deterministically.

For the 0x90-0x3F0s, what is lost are five checks (b90ddd08f6 in 2.29). A
chunk lands in smallbin without ever being inspected. It could be carrying
a forged SIZE. It could be pointing anywhere. Smallbin will never ask.

For both, the deeper tcache (7->16) reduces how often the arena path is
reached. Is this a mitigation?


### --[ 1 - A Taxonomy of the Small

In glibc, a chunk is "small" when `chunksize < 1024` bytes (0x400). Small
chunks are further divided by how they were treated before the fall:

```text
+------------+----------+-----------------+-----------------+--------------+
| Size Range | Bytes    | Pre-2.43 Path   | 2.43 Path       | What They    |
|            |          |                 |                 | Lost         |
+------------+----------+-----------------+-----------------+--------------+
| 0x20-0x80  | 32-128   | fastbin         | smallbin direct | PROTECT_PTR  |
|            |          |                 |                 | + size check |
+------------+----------+-----------------+-----------------+--------------+
| 0x90-0x3F0 | 144-1008 | unsorted->small | smallbin direct | unsorted 5   |
|            |          | bin             |                 | checks       |
+------------+----------+-----------------+-----------------+--------------+
```


#### ----[ 1.1 - The Seven Smallest

Seven sizes. That is all. 32 to 128 bytes. `malloc(1)` through
`malloc(120)`. The 0x20 holds a single pointer and a short string. The
0x40 holds a `sockaddr`. The 0x80 holds a modest pathname. They are the
most common allocations in any Unix process.

```text
+------------+------------+------------------------+-----------------------+
| Chunk Size | csize      | malloc() Range         | Smallbin Index        |
+------------+------------+------------------------+-----------------------+
| 0x20       | 24         | malloc(1)-malloc(24)   | 0                     |
+------------+------------+------------------------+-----------------------+
| 0x30       | 40         | malloc(25)-malloc(40)  | 1                     |
+------------+------------+------------------------+-----------------------+
| 0x40       | 56         | malloc(41)-malloc(56)  | 2                     |
+------------+------------+------------------------+-----------------------+
| 0x50       | 72         | malloc(57)-malloc(72)  | 3                     |
+------------+------------+------------------------+-----------------------+
| 0x60       | 88         | malloc(73)-malloc(88)  | 4                     |
+------------+------------+------------------------+-----------------------+
| 0x70       | 104        | malloc(89)-malloc(104) | 5                     |
+------------+------------+------------------------+-----------------------+
| 0x80       | 120        | malloc(105)-malloc(120)| 6                     |
+------------+------------+------------------------+-----------------------+
```

In pre-2.43, when tcache was full, these seven went through the fastbin.
They had PROTECT_PTR on `fd`, XOR-mangled, position-dependent, forcing the
attacker to know where the chunk lived before forging a pointer. They had
a size-index check at retrieval: `fastbin_index(chunksize(victim)) ==
idx`. If the SIZE didn't match the bin, malloc refused to return it.

In 2.43 they go to smallbin directly. `fd` and `bk` are raw. No mangling.
No size check. Just `bck->fd == victim`, one backward-link check. That is
all that stands between them and the attacker.


#### ----[ 1.2 - The Fifty-Five in the Middle

The 0x90 through 0x3F0 range, 55 sizes, 144 to 1008 bytes. They never had
PROTECT_PTR on the arena path. But they did pass through the unsorted bin,
and the unsorted bin checked them. Five checks. Five small questions. Is
your size in bounds? Is your neighbor's size in bounds? Does your neighbor
agree about your size? Are your list pointers consistent? Are you marked
free? And if any answer was wrong, glibc stopped them. In 2.43, glibc
stopped asking.


### --[ 2 - The Old Fences


#### ----[ 2.1 - Safe-Linking

```c
#define PROTECT_PTR(pos, ptr) \
  ((__typeof (ptr)) ((((size_t) pos) >> 12) ^ ((size_t) ptr)))
```

Simple. Elegant. Every `next` pointer is XOR'd with its own address
shifted right by 12. To forge a pointer, you must know where the chunk
lives.  It applied to both tcache `next` and fastbin `fd` since 2.32. In
2.43 it protects tcache only.

Fastbin's PROTECT_PTR died with fastbin.
The 0x20 through 0x80 lost their only structural `fd` protection. They are
protected in tcache, but doubly-linked with raw pointers in smallbin. No
masking. No obfuscation. Nothing.


#### ----[ 2.2 - Safe-Unlinking

Doubly-linked lists have unlink checks. They come in two strengths.

Full safe-unlinking: Applied during consolidation and largebin
retrieval (`unlink_chunk`):

```c
if (chunksize(p) != prev_size(next_chunk(p)))
    corrupt("corrupted size vs. prev_size");
if (FD->bk != p || BK->fd != p)
    corrupt("corrupted double-linked list");
```

Both directions. Both neighbors must agree. If you corrupt a chunk in a bin
that uses full safe-unlinking, you had better have a plan.

Partial safe-unlinking: Applied during smallbin retrieval:

```c
bck = victim->bk;
if (bck->fd != victim)
    corrupt("...corrupted");
bin->bk = bck;
bck->fd = bin;
```

One direction. Only `bk->fd`. Check the backward pointer's forward pointer
points back. That is it. And then it writes a libc address into `bck->fd`
anyway. The stash loop, which can return up to 16 chunks into tcache, does
not even do this much. It performs no integrity checks at all. It just
pulls.


### --[ 3 - What Was Lost


#### ----[ 3.1 - 0x20-0x80: The Fallen Seven

Before 2.43, the seven sizes from 0x20 to 0x80 lived in fastbin. They had
three protections, all three are gone.

PROTECT_PTR on `fd`. Gone. The mangling that made blind read/write
corruption unreliable, that forced the attacker to know the chunk's
position before forging `fd`, is no longer applied. `fd` and `bk` are raw
pointers. A blind corruption needs no heap leak. Unsafe_unlink needs no
position reconstruction. The chunk sits there with its pointers exposed.

The size-index check. Gone. Fastbin retrieval verified
`fastbin_index(chunksize(victim)) != idx`. A post-free SIZE corruption,
inflating a 0x20 chunk to appear as 0x40, was caught on the way out.
Smallbin retrieval has no such check. It does not look at SIZE. It does
not call `set_head`. A corrupted SIZE survives retrieval intact, enabling
inflate-after-free and overlapping-chunk attacks.

The early return in `_int_free`. Gone. Before 2.43, fastbin's early
return meant `_int_free_merge_chunk` was never reached for
these sizes. `unlink_chunk` was only reachable via `malloc_consolidate`,
triggered by a large malloc, and itself validating 
`fastbin_index(chunksize(p))` against the source fastbin. In 2.43, every
arena free enters the full merge path. `clear_inuse_bit_at_offset`
auto-clears the next chunk's PREV_INUSE. No SIZE validation. No guard.

And there is a fourth thing. Fastbin was singly-linked. There was no `bk`.
Now there is. The `bk` pointer in smallbin is an allocation-control
vector: corrupt it, and House of Lore returns arbitrary chunks; corrupt it
during stashing, and one corruption propagates into up to 16 tcache
entries. Fastbin gave one extra return per forged entry, smallbin
amplifies.


#### ----[ 3.2 - 0x90-0x3F0: The Five Lost Questions

Before 2.41, chunks of this size passed through the unsorted bin. The
unsorted-bin traversal in `_int_malloc` asked five questions of every
chunk removed:

1. Is your `size` within bounds?
2. Is your neighbor's `size` within bounds?
3. Does your neighbor's `prev_size` match your `size`?
4. Do both your `fd` and `bk` list pointers agree?
5. Are you marked free?

After 2.41, chunks go directly to smallbin via `_int_free_create_chunk`.
None of the five questions are asked. Not one.

A chunk with a corrupted SIZE would have triggered check 3 during
unsorted-bin removal. Now it lands in smallbin unchecked, the corrupted
SIZE persists, enabling overlapping-chunk attacks on all 55 smallbin sizes.

A doubly-linked-list corruption in unsorted bin would have triggered check
4. Now smallbin retrieval checks only one direction (`bck->fd`), 
and the stash loop checks none. 

Now chunks enter smallbin directly. A chunk can sit in smallbin with live
neighbors, live neighbors whose metadata can be corrupted, whose PREV_INUSE
flags can be toggled, whose consolidation can be triggered at will.


### --[ 4 - Overlapping Little Chunks

Sections 1-3 laid out the losses. Little chunks lost PROTECT_PTR, lost the
size-index check at bin retrieval, gained a deterministic consolidation
path with no SIZE validation. In plain terms, dear reader, 2.43 protects
little chunks less.

And so, as a direct result, all three overlapping-chunk approaches from
"Glibc adventures: The forgotten chunks" (Goichon, 2015) are now enabled
for little chunks. All three. This was not the case before 2.43, the 0x20s
and 0x80s were safe. Now they are not.

We use overlapping chunks here for three reasons. First, it lets us
introduce sleeping corruption, as an exploitation pattern. Neither
tcache nor smallbin inspects the SIZE field. `tcache_put` writes `e->next`
and `e->key`. `tcache_get` clears `e->key`. Smallbin exact-fit retrieval
checks only `bckfd`. Neither calls `set_head`. Neither reads the SIZE
field for validation. A chunk can be freed, its SIZE corrupted after free
while it rests peacefully in either bin, then retrieved and freed again at
the forged SIZE. The bin never sees the corruption. It sleeps right
through it. The chunk slumbers with a lie written on its forehead, and
nobody notices.

Second, the three approaches map cleanly onto the Forgotten Chunks
framework, making the parallel explicit. And third, walking through them
clarifies overlapping-chunk concepts that are often glossed over.

But let us be clear: overlapping chunks is just one example for these
sizes. The 2.43 changes enable other primitives for 0x20-0x80 and
0x90-0x3F0 that kick in after tcache on the arena path. Two of the three
approaches also work from tcache. Forward consolidation requires the arena
path and is specifically enabled by 2.43. There could be worse things these
changes enable. We chose this one because it is clean, because it lets us
introduce sleeping corruption, and because it maps beautifully onto
Forgotten Chunks. But it could be only the beginning.


#### ----[ 4.1 - Sleeping Corruption

Tcache is the first stop for every little chunk on free. `__libc_free`
checks tcache before any arena path. This was true before 2.43 and remains
true after. And here is the quiet miracle: `tcache_put` / `tcache_get`
never touch SIZE. `tcache_put` writes `e->next` and `e->key`. `tcache_get`
clears `e->key`. Neither calls `set_head`. The SIZE field survives
completely untouched through any number of tcache put/get cycles. It is as
if the chunk passed through a room where no one looked at its papers.
Sleeping corruption in tcache has always been possible for little chunks.

The arena path, what happens when tcache reaches capacity, lost its guards 
in two steps.

For 0x20-0x80, pre-2.43, the journey was guarded, the chunk fell through
to `_int_free` and landed in fastbin. Fastbin retrieval checked
`fastbin_index(chunksize(victim)) != idx`. A SIZE mismatch was caught. The
corruption was awakened, exposed, and the process blocked.

For 0x90-0x3F0, pre-2.41, the chunk landed in unsorted bin. On the next
`malloc` traversal, b90ddd08f6 check 3 validated SIZE. Again, the corruption
was caught. Sleeping corruption through the arena path was blocked for both
size classes.

Post-2.43, both take the same path: tcache -> smallbin directly. Smallbin
exact-fit retrieval checks only `bckfd == victim`. No SIZE validation. The
sleeping corruption survives through the arena path for both size classes.
The chunk arrives in smallbin with its forged SIZE still intact. It is
retrieved with the forged SIZE. It is freed again with the forged SIZE.
The corruption was never detected because nobody ever looked.

Bins where sleeping corruption works:

- tcache: `tcache_put`/`tcache_get` never call `set_head`. SIZE
untouched.
- smallbin: Exact-fit retrieval has no SIZE check. Only `bckfd`
checked.

Bins where it does not:

- fastbin: `fastbin_index(chunksize(victim)) != idx` at retrieval.
- unsorted bin: b90ddd08f6 checks 3 validate SIZE and neighbor
integrity.
- largebin: Full safe-unlinking, bin-fit checks.

Sleeping corruption converts a post-free corruption into an effective
pre-free state. This is the alchemy:

```text
free(A)              -> chunk is free in tcache/smallbin
... corrupt A->size = INFLATED  -> SIZE corruption is sleeping
malloc(A)            -> retrieved, allocated chunk has forged SIZE
free(A)              -> _int_free reads INFLATED SIZE 
```

The corruption sleeps while the chunk rests in the bin. It activates only
at the second free, converting a post-free corruption into what is
functionally a pre-free corruption. Sleeping corruption
delivers that illusion, perfectly, because the bin never validated the
SIZE in the first place.


#### ----[ 4.2 - Three Approaches

The Forgotten Chunks paper identified three sub-techniques
for creating overlapping chunks via SIZE-field corruption. With sleeping
corruption, all three now work for little chunks in glibc 2.43. All three.
Let that sink in.

```text
+-------+------------------+------------------------+----------------------+
| #     | Forgotten        | Approach               | Works on little      |
|       | Chunks           |                        | chunks?              |
+-------+------------------+------------------------+----------------------+
| 1     | Extending        | Before-free. Corrupt   | Yes, any glibc       |
|       | Allocated        | SIZE while the chunk   | version, both size   |
|       |                  | is allocated. free()   | classes, the         |
|       |                  | routes it at the       | classic.             |
|       |                  | inflated SIZE.         |                      |
|       |                  | malloc(inflated)       |                      |
|       |                  | returns the            |                      |
|       |                  | overlapping region.    |                      |
+-------+------------------+------------------------+----------------------+
| 2     | Extending Free   | After-free with        | Yes, tcache always,  |
|       |                  | sleeping corruption.   | the arena path now   |
|       |                  | Free the chunk,        | goes to smallbin,    |
|       |                  | corrupt its SIZE while | with no SIZE check   |
|       |                  | it sleeps in tcache or | on retrieval.        |
|       |                  | smallbin, retrieve it, |                      |
|       |                  | free it at the         |                      |
|       |                  | inflated SIZE.         |                      |
|       |                  | malloc(inflated)       |                      |
|       |                  | returns the            |                      |
|       |                  | overlapping region.    |                      |
+-------+------------------+------------------------+----------------------+
| 3     | Shrinking Free   | Forgotten chunk via    | Yes, but arena only, |
|       |                  | forward consolidation. | never tcache         |
|       |                  | Free B into tcache;    | (tcache_put returns  |
|       |                  | inflate B's own SIZE   | before the merge     |
|       |                  | while it sleeps so     | path). New for       |
|       |                  | nextchunk(B) reaches   | 0x20-0x80 in 2.43    |
|       |                  | past the live T to W.  | (fastbin gone); for  |
|       |                  | Retrieve B (forged     | 0x90-0x3F0 already   |
|       |                  | SIZE survives). Fill   | reachable.           |
|       |                  | tcache, free W ->      |                      |
|       |                  | smallbin (free). Free  |                      |
|       |                  | B again -> arena merge |                      |
|       |                  | path forward-          |                      |
|       |                  | consolidates B+W via   |                      |
|       |                  | unlink_chunk,          |                      |
|       |                  | swallowing T.          |                      |
|       |                  | malloc(merged) returns |                      |
|       |                  | B, overlapping T.      |                      |
+-------+------------------+------------------------+----------------------+
```

Approach 1: Before-free. The classic. The `overlapping_chunks.c`
pattern everyone knows. Corrupt SIZE while the chunk is allocated. Then
free it. `free()` and `_int_malloc` both read the same corrupted SIZE, so
they agree. This has always worked for little chunks.

Approach 2: After-free with sleeping corruption. In tcache it always worked,
but capacity 7 limited how deep you could go. Post-2.43, the arena path for
both goes to smallbin, which neither checks SIZE nor calls `set_head`.
Sleeping corruption now survives from tcache through the arena bin
uninterrupted. What was a shallow pool is now an ocean.

Approach 3: Forgotten chunk via forward consolidation. This parallels
Shrinking Free Chunks but through forward consolidation rather than
unsorted-bin sub-allocation. Chunk B is freed into tcache. Its own SIZE is
corrupted while sleeping. B is retrieved, and its forged SIZE is left
intact. W is freed and lands free in smallbin. B is freed a second time,
if its corrupted SIZE no longer lands into tcache, enters 
`_int_free_merge_chunk`, finds W free and `unlink_chunk` merges B+W, 
swallowing the live T. A malloc of the merged size returns B, overlapping T.
Note, it is the size corruption that covers T, B's inflated size already
spans it, consolidation swallows W, and launders the forgery into an honest
size. Pre-2.43 this was impossible for 0x20-0x80, fastbin early return kept
the merge path unreachable. For 0x90-0x3F0 chunks, which have reached the
arena merge path, the post-free path was already available.


#### ----[ 4.3 - Why It Matters

When a little chunk left tcache and hit the arena path, it landed in a bin 
that validated SIZE: fastbin for the seven smallest (checked
`fastbin_index(chunksize(victim)) != idx`), unsorted bin for the fifty-five
in the middle (b90ddd08f6 check 3). Both blocked post-free SIZE corruption.
Both caught the lie. Post-2.43, both land in smallbin, which does not check
SIZE. Does not call `set_head`. Does not care.

```text
+----------------------------+--------------------+---------------------+
| Approach                   | Pre-2.43           | Post-2.43           |
+----------------------------+--------------------+---------------------+
| Before-free (Extending     | YES                | YES                 |
| Allocated)                 |                    |                     |
+----------------------------+--------------------+---------------------+
| After-free (Extending      | YES (tcache only,  | YES (tcache +       |
| Free)                      | capacity 7)        | smallbin)           |
+----------------------------+--------------------+---------------------+
| Forgotten chunk (Forward   | NO (0x20-0x80      | YES (post-free via  |
| Consolidation)             | blocked;           | sleeping            |
|                            | 0x90-0x3F0         | corruption)         |
|                            | indirect pre-free  |                     |
|                            | only)              |                     |
+----------------------------+--------------------+---------------------+
```

The net effect is simple: the arena path changed from bins that validate
SIZE to one that does not. Yes, tcache capacity doubled but little chunks
in arena lost their SIZE-validating safety net.

And so, dear reader, this is where they stand: the 0x20s, the 0x40s, the
brave 0x90s. Fastbin is gone. The unsorted checks are gone. PROTECT_PTR is
gone. The seven smallest, stripped of their mangled `fd` and their
size-index check. The fifty-five middle, stripped of their five validation
questions. The tcache doubled, keeping more of them from ever reaching the
arena, but the ones that do, they wander into smallbin unchecked. They
carry forged SIZE fields and nobody inspects them. They sleep with
corrupted metadata and nobody wakes them. Three approaches to overlapping
chunks, all three now working. Sleeping corruption, what was always possible
in tcache is now possible everywhere.

This is grief, dear reader, look at what they left behind. And again, this
is only one of the places where the loss shows its weight.

PoCs attached. The proof is left where it belongs.
